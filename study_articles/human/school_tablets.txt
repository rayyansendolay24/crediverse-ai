School district pauses tablet rollout after pilot shows mixed results

The county school district is pausing its plan to issue tablets to every middle school student after a pilot program at three schools produced mixed results on reading and math scores, district officials said Thursday.

The pilot, run last school year at Eastview, Hilltop, and Carver middle schools, gave roughly 1,800 students district-issued tablets loaded with reading and math software. End-of-year test data showed reading scores roughly flat compared to non-pilot schools, while math scores rose about 4 percentage points at two of the three schools but fell slightly at the third.

Superintendent Linda Ortiz told the school board the district needs more time to understand why results varied so much by school before expanding the $6.8 million program districtwide.

"We're not abandoning this, we're slowing down," Ortiz said. "A rollout this size needs to work everywhere, not just at two out of three schools."

Teachers at Carver, where math scores dipped, told district researchers that spotty wifi in some classrooms limited how often the software could be used, a problem the other two schools did not report.

The district will spend the coming semester upgrading wifi infrastructure at under-performing pilot sites and re-testing before deciding whether to expand the program next fall. A board vote on the expanded rollout is now expected in the spring rather than this winter as originally planned.
