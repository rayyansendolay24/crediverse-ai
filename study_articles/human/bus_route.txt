City council approves new cross-town bus route after two years of hearings

The city council voted 6-2 on Tuesday night to approve a new express bus route linking the eastern suburbs to the downtown business district, ending nearly two years of public hearings and budget wrangling.

Route 14X will run every 20 minutes during peak hours starting in March, with dedicated bus lanes along a 4.3-kilometre stretch of Grant Avenue. Transit officials estimate the route will cut commute times for roughly 8,000 daily riders by up to 15 minutes.

"We've heard from residents in these neighbourhoods for years that getting downtown without a car takes almost an hour," said transit authority spokesperson Maria Covington at Tuesday's meeting. "This route was built directly from their feedback."

Not everyone was satisfied. Two council members who voted against the measure cited the $4.2 million cost of the dedicated lanes, arguing the money would be better spent repaving existing roads. A small group of shop owners along Grant Avenue also raised concerns about construction disruption to foot traffic.

Construction on the dedicated lanes is expected to begin in January and take six to eight weeks. The transit authority says temporary signage and detour routes will be posted at least two weeks before work starts.

The route was first proposed in a 2024 transit equity study that found eastern suburb residents faced the longest average commute times in the city.
