Regional wheat harvest down 12% after dry spring, farmers group reports

Wheat yields across the county fell about 12 percent this season compared to the five-year average, according to figures released Monday by the regional farmers cooperative, as a dry spring and a late frost in April took a toll on crops.

The cooperative's survey of 140 member farms found average yields of 3.1 tonnes per hectare, down from 3.5 tonnes last year. Cooperative chair Harold Mensah said the shortfall was most severe in the northern part of the county, where rainfall in March and April was roughly 40 percent below normal.

"It wasn't one bad week, it was a bad stretch of six or seven weeks right when the crop needed water most," Mensah said.

Despite lower yields, many farmers are expected to see similar or slightly higher revenue this year because wheat futures prices have risen about 8 percent since June, partly in response to reduced production forecasts across the wider region.

Not all farms were affected equally. Operations using drip irrigation, which now covers about a third of the county's wheat acreage, reported yields close to normal. Mensah said the cooperative is pushing a cost-sharing grant program to help more farmers install irrigation before next season.

The county's full harvest data will be finalized in a state agriculture department report expected in November.
