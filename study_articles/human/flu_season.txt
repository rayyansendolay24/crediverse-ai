Health department urges early flu shots as cases tick up earlier than usual

The county health department is urging residents to get flu vaccinations now, after reporting a 30 percent rise in confirmed influenza cases over the past two weeks, a jump officials say is arriving about three weeks earlier than typical flu seasons.

As of Monday, the department had logged 214 confirmed cases countywide, compared with 164 at the same point last year. Most cases so far have been among school-age children, with two local elementary schools reporting absence rates above 15 percent last week.

Dr. Priya Sharma, the county's deputy health officer, said the early uptick doesn't necessarily mean a worse season overall, but it does mean people should not wait until December to get vaccinated as many usually do.

"The vaccine takes about two weeks to reach full effectiveness. If you wait until cases are already everywhere, you've lost that window," Sharma said.

Free flu shot clinics will run at six county locations through the end of November, with extended hours added at the downtown clinic on weekends starting this Saturday. The department says this year's vaccine is a strong match for the circulating strains based on early genetic sequencing data, though officials cautioned that match estimates can shift as the season progresses.

Residents with flu symptoms are advised to stay home for at least 24 hours after their fever breaks without medication.
