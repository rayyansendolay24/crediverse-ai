Upgraded water treatment plant cuts lead testing failures by half, city says

The city's main water treatment plant has reduced lead-level test failures by roughly 50 percent since a $12 million filtration upgrade was completed last spring, according to figures released by the public works department this week.

Of the 340 household samples tested across the city in the third quarter, 9 exceeded the federal action level for lead, down from 19 failures in the same period last year. Public works director Tomas Reyes attributed the improvement to new corrosion-control chemicals added at the plant in April, which coat aging pipes and reduce the amount of lead that leaches into tap water.

"We're not declaring victory yet," Reyes said at Wednesday's press briefing. "Nine households is still nine households too many, and most of those failures are tracing back to lead service lines on private property that we don't control."

The city has budgeted $3.5 million next year to replace an estimated 600 remaining lead service lines, prioritizing homes in the Westgate and Millbrook neighbourhoods, where lead pipes are most common due to the age of housing stock.

Residents can request a free water test kit through the public works website. The department says results typically take two to three weeks.

The upgrade was funded through a mix of state infrastructure grants and a small increase in water utility rates approved by voters in 2023.
