New Bus Route Approved to Improve City Transit Connectivity

In a significant development for local transportation infrastructure, the city council has approved a new bus route designed to connect the eastern suburbs with the downtown business district. The decision, which passed by a vote of 6 to 2, comes after an extensive two-year period of public hearings and discussions regarding budget allocation.

The newly approved route, designated 14X, is set to begin operations in March. It is important to note that the route will operate at 20-minute intervals during peak commuting hours, utilizing dedicated bus lanes spanning approximately 4.3 kilometres along Grant Avenue. According to transit officials, this initiative is expected to benefit around 8,000 daily commuters by reducing their travel time by as much as 15 minutes.

Transit authority representatives have emphasized that this route reflects direct community feedback. As one official noted, residents in the affected neighbourhoods have long expressed concerns about the challenges of commuting downtown without access to a personal vehicle.

However, it should be mentioned that the decision was not without controversy. Two council members voted against the proposal, citing the significant cost of $4.2 million associated with constructing the dedicated lanes. In addition, several local business owners expressed apprehension about potential disruptions to foot traffic during the construction phase.

Looking ahead, construction is anticipated to commence in January and is projected to last between six and eight weeks. Overall, this route builds upon findings from a 2024 transit equity study, which identified eastern suburb residents as experiencing the city's longest average commute times. In conclusion, this development represents a meaningful step toward addressing long-standing transportation inequities within the city.
