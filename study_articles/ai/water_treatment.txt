City's Water Treatment Upgrade Leads to Significant Reduction in Lead Test Failures

The city's public works department has announced encouraging results following a major upgrade to its water treatment infrastructure. According to newly released data, lead-level test failures have decreased by approximately 50 percent since the completion of a $12 million filtration system upgrade last spring.

Specifically, out of 340 household samples collected during the third quarter, only 9 were found to exceed the federal action level for lead. This marks a notable improvement compared to the 19 failures recorded during the same period in the previous year. Public works director Tomas Reyes attributed this positive outcome to the introduction of new corrosion-control chemicals at the treatment plant in April. These chemicals work by forming a protective coating on aging pipes, thereby minimizing the amount of lead that can enter the water supply.

Despite this progress, officials have been careful to temper expectations. "We're not declaring victory yet," Reyes stated during a press briefing held on Wednesday, noting that the remaining failures are largely linked to lead service lines located on private property, which fall outside the city's direct control.

Looking forward, the city has allocated $3.5 million for the coming year to replace an estimated 600 remaining lead service lines. This effort will prioritize the Westgate and Millbrook neighbourhoods, areas identified as having a higher concentration of lead pipes due to the age of their housing stock.

It is worth noting that residents have the option to request a complimentary water test kit via the public works website, with results generally available within two to three weeks. This infrastructure upgrade was made possible through a combination of state infrastructure grants and a modest increase in water utility rates, which voters approved back in 2023. Overall, this initiative reflects a continued commitment to improving public health outcomes through infrastructure investment.
