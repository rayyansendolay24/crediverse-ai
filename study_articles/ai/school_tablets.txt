School District Pauses Tablet Rollout Following Mixed Pilot Program Results

The county school district has announced a pause in its plan to distribute tablets to all middle school students, following a pilot program that yielded mixed results in terms of reading and math performance. This decision was communicated by district officials on Thursday.

The pilot program, which took place during the last school year, was implemented at three schools: Eastview, Hilltop, and Carver middle schools. Approximately 1,800 students received district-issued tablets equipped with reading and math software as part of this initiative. According to end-of-year test data, reading scores remained largely unchanged when compared to schools not involved in the pilot. Meanwhile, math scores showed an increase of about 4 percentage points at two of the three participating schools, though a slight decline was observed at the third school.

Superintendent Linda Ortiz addressed the school board, emphasizing the need for additional time to better understand the variation in results across different schools before considering a districtwide expansion of the $6.8 million program.

"We're not abandoning this, we're slowing down," Ortiz stated, adding that a program of this scale must demonstrate effectiveness across all participating schools, not merely a subset.

It is worth noting that teachers at Carver, where math scores experienced a decline, reported to district researchers that inconsistent wifi connectivity in certain classrooms limited the frequency with which the software could be utilized. This particular issue was not reported at the other two pilot schools.

Moving forward, the district plans to dedicate the upcoming semester to improving wifi infrastructure at the underperforming pilot locations, followed by additional testing to inform future decisions regarding program expansion. As a result, the school board's vote on the broader rollout has been rescheduled to occur in the spring, rather than during the winter as originally intended.
